On hosted vibecms every blog starts on the free plan, free forever. The free plan includes:

- 25 published posts
- 10 drafts at a time
- 100 images and 250 MB of media, whichever is reached first
- A custom domain (one per blog) or the blog under `/blog` of your own site, plus `name.vibecms.dev`
- Open to search engines and AI assistants: pages, sitemap, robots.txt, feeds, and llms.txt are the same as on Cloud
- Analytics for the last 7 days: readers and agent reads per day, site totals only
- Agent keys, with 300 requests a day and 1,000 a month; writes are limited to 50 a day and 200 a month
- Unlimited version history, themes, SEO checks, redirects, and JSON export
- Standard support
- A small "Published with vibecms" link at the bottom of every blog page

vibecms Cloud includes unlimited posts and drafts, 10 GB of media, full analytics (all history, per-post numbers, referrers, and agent breakdowns), no "Published with vibecms" link, priority support, and 5,000 agent requests a day. Agent requests are limited to 120 a minute, writes to 500 a day, and each key to 60 requests a minute, with no monthly cap (see [API limits](/docs/api/authentication#limits)). Address options are the same on both plans. Self-hosted installs never show the link. Prices live on the [pricing section](https://vibecms.dev/#pricing) of the site and are not repeated here.

## Upgrade and manage

Open **Dashboard > Settings > Plan & billing**. The owner sees the current plan, the full allowance table, and **Upgrade** / **Yearly** checkout buttons. Once subscribed, **Manage subscription** opens the billing portal for payment details and cancellation; **Invoices and payment details** appears for past customers. Leaving checkout or the portal brings you back to Plan & billing. Checkout and the portal are owner-only actions — editors see "Only the owner can change the plan."

Each blog has its own subscription. One person can pay for several blogs with the same email; the billing portal then lists every subscription paid with that email, including other products from the same seller. If one blog is paid for twice by mistake (for example two checkouts open in two tabs), it stays on the first subscription and support refunds the second.

## Getting close and reaching a limit

From 20 published posts or 80 images, the dashboard Overview shows one line with what is left ("5 posts left on Free") and **Get Cloud**. Dismissing it keeps it away for that blog. The agent hears it once too: the publish that brings the blog to 20 posts, or the upload that brings it to 80 images, returns a `planNotice` to pass on to the owner: how many are left and that everything stays online. It names no plan and has no link.

When a free limit refuses a publish, a draft or an upload, the place where it failed says the limit, that published posts stay online and editable, and offers **Get Cloud**: one click opens checkout (owner only). In the editor it opens in a new tab, so unsaved writing stays put. The free Analytics screen says what Cloud adds next to the same button.

## Canceling and failed payments

Canceling in the billing portal schedules the end of the subscription: the plan stays paid until the end of the current billing period, and Plan & billing shows **Ends** with that date instead of **Renews**. After it ends, the free plan applies and checkout is available again.

If a renewal payment fails, the subscription is past due and paid features stay on for a 7-day grace period from the first failed payment. Every dashboard page shows "Your last payment failed. Paid features stay on until _date_." with **Update payment**, which opens the billing portal to fix the payment method; editors see a note to ask the owner instead. Starting a new checkout is not offered while a payment is past due.

The grace clock starts with the first accepted past-due notification and does not restart on repeated failures. If notifications are delayed while our endpoint is failing, the clock can start later, extending grace by at most that delay. Older or duplicate notifications never change the current clock. Legacy past-due subscriptions with an unknown failure date receive no retroactive grace. An accepted successful payment, including a completed checkout, ends that past-due episode; a later failed payment starts a new grace period.

If the payment is still failing when the grace period ends, the free plan applies (see below) and the notice changes to "Your last payment failed. Paid features are paused." Nothing is deleted or taken offline. Once the payment goes through, the subscription is active again and paid features return immediately. A subscription that ends — canceled, unpaid after the retries run out, or revoked — drops to the free plan with no grace period.

## When paid access ends

Nothing is deleted, unpublished, or hidden from search engines. Every published post stays online and editable, the custom domain and `/blog` location keep serving, and the blog stays open to search engines and AI assistants. The free plan applies again: the "Published with vibecms" link returns, analytics show the last 7 days of site totals, and new publishes, drafts, and uploads are refused until the blog is back under the free limits or on Cloud. Posts over the free published limit stay published; the cap only blocks *new* publishes past 25. Editing and republishing a post that is already live always works.

Free-plan limits fail with distinct codes — the same over REST and in the MCP tool error result: publishing past 25 posts or adding a draft past 10 → `BILLING_REQUIRED`; per-post analytics (`postId`) → `ANALYTICS_PAID_PLAN`; uploading past 100 images or 250 MB → `media_quota_free`; uploading past paid storage → `media_quota_paid`. Each message says the limit, that existing posts stay online and editable, that vibecms Cloud removes the limit, and tells the agent not to retry but to tell the blog owner, with the pricing link. Resubscribing lifts the limits at once.
